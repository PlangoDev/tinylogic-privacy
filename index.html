Tiny Logic privacy policy

Tiny Logic is a pixel circuit sandbox by Mazell Smack-Dubose. Last updated October 2026 (version 1.1.0).

The short version: Tiny Logic sends nothing about you unless you say yes. If you're too young for your region, it never asks and never sends anything. You can change your mind any time in Settings.

When the game asks

The first time you start Tiny Logic it asks your age. That answer stays on your computer. What counts as old enough depends on where your computer says you are (the language and region settings of your system; the game does not look you up on the internet):

RegionOld enough to choose
United States13 or older
Everywhere else, or if the region is unknown16 or older

If you're younger than that, nothing is collected and nothing is sent: no stats, no crash reports. Everything else in the game works the same.

If you're old enough, anonymous stats are off unless you choose Share anonymous stats. Choosing No thanks is just as fine, and the game works exactly the same either way.

What is sent if you say yes, and why

Sent (only after a yes)

  • A random install ID made on your computer. It isn't linked to your name, email or anything else about you.
  • Your system: operating system and version, CPU model and cores, memory, graphics card, screen and window size, display count and refresh rate, UI scale, whether a touch screen is there, and (on Linux) X11 or Wayland.
  • The game version and language.
  • How the game is used: time spent in lessons, the sandbox, the chip studio and the assembly editor; lessons started, steps reached, hints used, quiz answers right or wrong, lessons finished or left; kinds of parts placed; chips made; programs loaded or assembled; projects created, opened and saved (counted, never named); settings changed; share codes copied or pasted (counted); the length of what you type into the part picker's search, never the words.
  • How well it runs: frames a second, slow frames, simulation speed, compile times, memory use, and the size of the board (counts of parts, wires and transistors).
  • Sessions: when a session starts and how long it lasts, days since you installed, time of day.
  • Your answers to the optional "get to know you" questions (age range, what you do, how you found the game, what you want to build). Every question can be skipped.
  • Your itch.io name, only if you choose to link it.

Why: to see what's fun, what's confusing and what's slow, so the right things get built and fixed next.

Never sent

  • Your name, email or any account details (except an itch.io name you chose to link).
  • Your projects, circuits, chips or their names.
  • Anything you type: search words, project names, notes, programs.
  • File names or folders on your computer.
  • Your location. The stats service is told not to work out where you are from your internet address.

Crash reports

If Tiny Logic crashes, the next time it starts it asks (only if you're old enough): Tiny Logic crashed last time. Send the crash report? with Yes, No or Always. A crash report says what kind of crash it was, the game version, and where in the game's code it stopped (a "stack trace"). File paths in it are cut down to file names so your user name never goes along. Nothing about your projects is in it.

Where it goes

Stats and crash reports go to PostHog, hosted in the United States (us.i.posthog.com). They're sent in small batches in the background; if you're offline they wait on your computer and go later. They're kept to improve the game and deleted when you ask.

Changing your mind

  • Settings > Stats > off stops all stats and crash reports at once. Anything still waiting on your computer is deleted.
  • Settings > Stats > ask again shows the first-start questions again (your age too).
  • Settings > itch.io link > off stops sending your itch.io name.

Deleting your data

Email dubosemazell90@gmail.com and ask. To find your data, include your install ID: it's the installId line in Tiny Logic's settings.json (macOS: ~/Library/Application Support/Mazell/TinyLogic/, Windows: %APPDATA%\Mazell\TinyLogic\, Linux: ~/.local/share/Mazell/TinyLogic/). If you can't find it, write anyway and say roughly when you played.

Discord status

If the Discord app is running on your computer, Tiny Logic can show what you're doing on your Discord profile (for example "Building in sandbox" and how many transistors your board has; never a project's name). The game only talks to the Discord app on your own computer, not to the internet. Whether it's shown, and to whom, is up to your Discord settings (Activity Privacy).

Update check

When it starts, Tiny Logic downloads one tiny file from this site (version.txt) to see if there's a newer version. Nothing about you is sent with it. Like any website, GitHub (which hosts this site) sees the internet address the request came from; see GitHub's privacy statement.
